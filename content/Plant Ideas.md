# Plant Ideas for Longmont, CO

Longmont is in USDA Hardiness Zone 5b/6a. It features a semi-arid, high plains climate with alkaline clay soils, hot summers, and cold, dry winters. The best plants to add to your yard will be drought-tolerant (water-wise) and adapted to alkaline soil. 

Here are some excellent additions to consider for your available space:

## Wildflowers

### 1. Blue Flax (Linum lewisii)
- **Summary:** A delicate native wildflower that produces masses of sky-blue flowers on slender stems. They open in the morning and often drop their petals by the afternoon heat, but produce new flowers daily.
- **When it Flowers:** Late Spring to Mid-Summer (May - July).
- **When to Plant:** Early Spring or Fall.
- **Care Guide:** 
  - **Light:** Full sun.
  - **Soil:** Very adaptable, thrives in poor, sandy, or rocky soils.
  - **Water:** Low. Highly drought tolerant once established.
  - **Maintenance:** Self-seeds readily. Cut back in late fall.

### 2. Chocolate Flower (Berlandiera lyrata)
- **Summary:** A unique, low-growing native wildflower whose yellow, daisy-like flowers genuinely smell like milk chocolate, especially in the morning.
- **When it Flowers:** Early Summer to Fall (June - September).
- **When to Plant:** Spring after last frost.
- **Care Guide:**
  - **Light:** Full sun.
  - **Soil:** Well-drained, dry soil.
  - **Water:** Low. Do not overwater.
  - **Maintenance:** Deadhead occasionally to keep it looking tidy.


### 3. Firecracker Penstemon (Penstemon eatonii)
- **Summary:** Striking, bright red tubular flowers on tall stalks. An absolute magnet for hummingbirds.
- **When it Flowers:** Early to mid-summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or Fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Needs excellent drainage, thrives in gravelly soils. Very low water.

### 4. Rocky Mountain Bee Plant (Cleome serrulata)
- **Summary:** A tall, branching native annual that produces pink/purple clusters of flowers. Reseeds itself reliably.
- **When it Flowers:** Mid-summer to early fall.
- **When to Plant:** Sow seeds in late fall or early spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Adaptable to poor soil. Low water.

### 5. Sulfur Buckwheat (Eriogonum umbellatum)
- **Summary:** A low-growing native mat-forming plant with bright yellow flower umbels that turn a beautiful rusty-red in autumn.
- **When it Flowers:** Summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or Fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Must have extremely well-drained, sandy/rocky soil. Highly drought tolerant.

### 6. Showy Milkweed (Asclepias speciosa)
- **Summary:** The essential host plant for Monarch butterflies. Produces fragrant, globe-like clusters of star-shaped pinkish flowers.
- **When it Flowers:** Summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or Fall (from seed/rhizome).
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Tolerates heavy clay. Moderate to low water. Can spread aggressively via rhizomes.

### 7. Palmer's Penstemon (Penstemon palmeri)
- **Summary:** One of the few scented penstemons. Huge, pale pink flowers with deep maroon stripes inside.
- **When it Flowers:** Late spring to early summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or Fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Requires excellent drainage. Very low water.

### 8. Prairie Coneflower (Ratibida columnifera)
- **Summary:** Also known as Mexican Hat. Distinctive flowers with drooping yellow/red petals and a prominent central cone.
- **When it Flowers:** Summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Adaptable to most soils. Low water.

### 9. Wild Bergamot (Monarda fistulosa)
- **Summary:** A native bee balm with lavender-pink flowers. Leaves are highly fragrant (smells like Earl Grey tea).
- **When it Flowers:** Mid-summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun to part shade. Tolerates clay. Moderate water (prefers slightly more moisture than desert natives).

### 10. Desert Globemallow (Sphaeralcea ambigua)
- **Summary:** Produces masses of cup-shaped, brilliant orange flowers against silvery-green foliage.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring and intermittently through summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Needs well-drained soil. Extremely drought tolerant.

### 11. Dotted Gayfeather (Liatris punctata)
- **Summary:** Native blazing star with deep taproots. Spikes of purple, fuzzy flowers bloom from the top down.
- **When it Flowers:** Late summer to early fall.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Thrives in poor, dry soil. Extremely drought tolerant.

### 12. Scarlet Gilia (Ipomopsis aggregata)
- **Summary:** A striking biennial native. The first year it forms a rosette; the second year it shoots up a tall stalk of bright red tubular flowers.
- **When it Flowers:** Summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring (seeds).
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Needs well-drained soil. Low water.

## Perennials

### 1. Sunset Hyssop (Agastache rupestris)
- **Summary:** A stunning perennial with fine, silvery-green foliage that smells like root beer or licorice. It sends up spikes of tubular orange and pink flowers that are irresistible to hummingbirds.
- **When it Flowers:** Late Summer to Fall (August - October).
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:**
  - **Light:** Full sun.
  - **Soil:** Must have excellent drainage; amend heavy clay with squeegee or gravel.
  - **Water:** Low. Water deeply but infrequently.
  - **Maintenance:** Leave stalks standing through winter for interest and protection; cut back to the ground in mid-spring.

### 2. Russian Sage (Perovskia atriplicifolia)
- **Summary:** A tough, woody perennial with silvery foliage and clouds of lavender-blue flowers. It is incredibly heat and drought tolerant and practically thrives on neglect.
- **When it Flowers:** Mid-Summer to Fall (July - October).
- **When to Plant:** Spring or early Fall.
- **Care Guide:**
  - **Light:** Full sun.
  - **Soil:** Tolerates poor, alkaline soil as long as it drains well.
  - **Water:** Low. 
  - **Maintenance:** Cut woody stems back to 6-12 inches in early spring before new growth begins.


### 3. Jupiter's Beard (Centranthus ruber)
- **Summary:** A bushy perennial producing dense clusters of star-shaped crimson or pink flowers.
- **When it Flowers:** Early summer to fall.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun to part shade. Thrives in poor, alkaline soils. Low water. Deadhead to prevent aggressive self-seeding.

### 4. Hardy Ice Plant (Delosperma cooperi)
- **Summary:** A succulent, mat-forming groundcover that produces blindingly bright magenta flowers.
- **When it Flowers:** Summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Must have excellent drainage (sandy/gravelly) to survive winter. Very low water.

### 5. Blue Avena Grass (Helictotrichon sempervirens)
- **Summary:** A striking clump-forming ornamental grass with stiff, steel-blue blades.
- **When it Flowers:** Early summer (produces wheat-like seed heads).
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Needs well-drained soil. Low water.

### 6. Little Bluestem (Schizachyrium scoparium)
- **Summary:** A native prairie grass that turns a stunning mahogany-red in the fall.
- **When it Flowers:** Late summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Adaptable to most soils. Low water.

### 7. Winecups (Callirhoe involucrata)
- **Summary:** A sprawling, mat-forming native perennial with deeply lobed leaves and magenta, cup-shaped flowers.
- **When it Flowers:** Summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Thrives in hot, dry areas. Very low water.

### 8. Catmint (Nepeta x faassenii)
- **Summary:** A tough, sprawling perennial with aromatic grayish-green foliage and abundant lavender-blue flowers. Loved by bees.
- **When it Flowers:** Early summer to fall.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Adaptable to poor soil. Low water. Shear back after first bloom for a second flush.

### 9. Red Birds in a Tree (Scrophularia macrantha)
- **Summary:** A rare native perennial from New Mexico. Flowers look exactly like tiny red birds perched on the stems.
- **When it Flowers:** Mid-summer to fall.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Needs well-drained soil. Low water.

### 10. Autumn Joy Sedum (Hylotelephium 'Herbstfreude')
- **Summary:** A reliable succulent perennial. Green broccoli-like flower heads slowly turn pink in late summer, then rusty-red in fall.
- **When it Flowers:** Late summer to fall.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Highly adaptable but needs decent drainage. Low water.

### 11. May Night Salvia (Salvia x sylvestris 'May Night')
- **Summary:** Deep violet-blue flower spikes. Outstanding performer in Colorado gardens.
- **When it Flowers:** Early summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Adaptable soil. Moderate to low water. Deadhead for continuous blooming.

### 12. Creeping Thyme (Thymus serpyllum)
- **Summary:** A tough, fragrant groundcover that can handle light foot traffic. Forms a dense mat of tiny pink/purple flowers.
- **When it Flowers:** Early summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Needs well-drained soil. Low water.

## Shrubs

### 1. Apache Plume (Fallugia paradoxa)
- **Summary:** A fascinating native shrub. It produces white, rose-like flowers that are quickly followed by feathery, pinkish-purple seed heads that look like plumes of smoke covering the plant.
- **When it Flowers:** Late Spring to Fall (May - September).
- **When to Plant:** Spring or Fall.
- **Care Guide:**
  - **Light:** Full sun.
  - **Soil:** Gravelly or rocky, extremely well-drained.
  - **Water:** Very low. Thrives in bone-dry conditions.
  - **Maintenance:** Prune lightly in late winter to shape.

### 2. Serviceberry (Amelanchier alnifolia)
- **Summary:** A large multi-stemmed native shrub (or small tree) offering four-season interest: white spring flowers, delicious edible purple berries in summer (tastes like a blueberry/almond mix), brilliant orange-red fall foliage, and smooth winter bark.
- **When it Flowers:** Early Spring (April - May).
- **When to Plant:** Spring or early Fall.
- **Care Guide:**
  - **Light:** Full sun to partial shade.
  - **Soil:** Adaptable, but prefers loamy, well-drained soil.
  - **Water:** Moderate. Needs more water while fruiting.
  - **Maintenance:** Prune after flowering to remove crossing branches or thin the interior.


### 3. Fernbush (Chamaebatiaria millefolium)
- **Summary:** A rugged native shrub with incredibly fragrant, fern-like sticky leaves and white clusters of flowers.
- **When it Flowers:** Mid-summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Needs well-drained soil. Extremely drought tolerant.

### 4. Leadplant (Amorpha canescens)
- **Summary:** A native prairie shrub with silvery foliage and striking spikes of purple flowers with bright orange stamens.
- **When it Flowers:** Summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Adaptable soil, deep taproot makes it very drought tolerant.

### 5. Curl-leaf Mountain Mahogany (Cercocarpus ledifolius)
- **Summary:** A large, evergreen native shrub. Unremarkable flowers, but produces amazing feathery, twisted seed tails in late summer that catch the light beautifully.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring (inconspicuous).
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Thrives in dry, rocky soils. Very low water.

### 6. Rabbitbrush (Ericameria nauseosa)
- **Summary:** A ubiquitous native shrub of the high desert. Explodes into brilliant yellow flowers in late summer when most other things are dying back.
- **When it Flowers:** Late summer to fall.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Poor, dry soil. Do not overwater or fertilize.

### 7. Golden Currant (Ribes aureum)
- **Summary:** A native shrub with spicy-scented yellow spring flowers, followed by edible berries (black, red, or yellow). Good fall color.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun to part shade. Adaptable soil. Moderate to low water.

### 8. Blue Arctic Willow (Salix purpurea 'Nana')
- **Summary:** A compact, rounded shrub with fine-textured, bluish-green foliage and purple stems in winter.
- **When it Flowers:** Catkins in early spring.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun to part shade. Adaptable soil. Moderate water (prefers more moisture than desert shrubs).

### 9. Gro-Low Fragrant Sumac (Rhus aromatica 'Gro-Low')
- **Summary:** A low, spreading shrub used as a woody groundcover. Excellent glossy green leaves turn brilliant red/orange in fall.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring (small yellow flowers).
- **When to Plant:** Spring or fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun to part shade. Adaptable to poor soil and drought.

### 10. Dwarf Korean Lilac (Syringa meyeri 'Palibin')
- **Summary:** A compact lilac that won't outgrow its space. Very prolific bloomer with highly fragrant pale purple flowers.
- **When it Flowers:** Late spring.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Well-drained soil. Moderate water. Prune immediately after blooming.

### 11. Pawnee Buttes Sandcherry (Prunus besseyi 'Pawnee Buttes')
- **Summary:** A low-growing, sprawling native shrub with fragrant white spring flowers and excellent red/purple fall color.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Very adaptable to poor soils and drought.

### 12. Mockorange (Philadelphus lewisii)
- **Summary:** A medium-sized native shrub famous for its masses of pure white flowers that smell intensely of citrus/orange blossoms.
- **When it Flowers:** Early summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun to part shade. Adaptable soil. Moderate water.

## Trees

### 1. Hackberry (Celtis occidentalis)
- **Summary:** An underutilized, incredibly tough shade tree. It handles Longmont's high winds, alkaline soils, and temperature swings much better than Maples or Ashes. Features interesting corky bark.
- **When it Flowers:** Inconspicuous green flowers in Spring.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or early Fall.
- **Care Guide:**
  - **Light:** Full sun.
  - **Soil:** Highly adaptable to clay, loam, and alkaline conditions.
  - **Water:** Moderate. Drought tolerant once established.
  - **Maintenance:** Prune in winter dormancy. Can be prone to harmless nipple gall on leaves.

### 2. Thornless Honeylocust (Gleditsia triacanthos inermis)
- **Summary:** A fast-growing shade tree with tiny leaflets that provide dappled shade (allowing grass to grow underneath). Turns a stunning golden-yellow in the fall. Leaves are so small they often don't require raking.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring (inconspicuous).
- **When to Plant:** Spring or early Fall.
- **Care Guide:**
  - **Light:** Full sun.
  - **Soil:** Extremely adaptable.
  - **Water:** Moderate to low once established.
  - **Maintenance:** Prune in late fall or winter. Susceptible to honeylocust plant bugs, but usually rebounds quickly.

### 3. Ponderosa Pine (Pinus ponderosa)
- **Summary:** The iconic native evergreen of the Colorado foothills. Distinctive long needles, large cones, and bark that smells like vanilla/butterscotch when warm.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring (cones).
- **When to Plant:** Spring or early fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Extremely drought tolerant once established. Needs space to grow large.

### 4. Kentucky Coffeetree (Gymnocladus dioicus)
- **Summary:** An incredibly tough, drought and pollution tolerant shade tree. Has huge compound leaves and interesting textured bark.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring (greenish-white).
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Highly adaptable to alkaline clay soils. Moderate to low water.

### 5. Bur Oak (Quercus macrocarpa)
- **Summary:** A majestic, long-lived shade tree with massive, fringed acorns. One of the best oaks for Colorado because it tolerates alkaline soil without getting chlorosis.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring (catkins).
- **When to Plant:** Spring or fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Highly adaptable. Moderate water, drought tolerant once mature.

### 6. Northern Redbud (Cercis canadensis)
- **Summary:** A small ornamental tree. Before leaves emerge in spring, the branches are covered in spectacular magenta-pink pea-like flowers. (Must buy a cold-hardy 'Northern' strain for CO).
- **When it Flowers:** Early spring.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Part shade to full sun. Needs well-drained soil. Moderate water.

### 7. Japanese Tree Lilac (Syringa reticulata)
- **Summary:** A small to medium tree that produces massive, creamy-white, fragrant flower panicles in early summer (later than shrub lilacs). Attractive cherry-like bark.
- **When it Flowers:** Early summer.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Well-drained soil. Moderate water.

### 8. Colorado Blue Spruce (Picea pungens)
- **Summary:** Our state tree. A dense, conical evergreen with striking silvery-blue needles.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring (cones).
- **When to Plant:** Spring or early fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Prefers richer, slightly moister soil than pines, but is adaptable.

### 9. Autumn Blaze Maple (Acer x freemanii)
- **Summary:** A fast-growing hybrid maple bred for spectacular, reliable, blazing red fall color. Much better suited for Colorado than traditional red maples.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Prone to iron chlorosis in highly alkaline soils. Moderate to high water.

### 10. Western Hackberry (Celtis reticulata)
- **Summary:** A smaller, native cousin of the common hackberry. Extremely rugged, drought-tolerant, and provides excellent habitat for birds.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring.
- **When to Plant:** Spring.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Thrives in poor, rocky, alkaline soils. Very low water.

### 11. Cockspur Hawthorn (Crataegus crus-galli inermis)
- **Summary:** A small, tough ornamental tree with white spring flowers, red berries in fall, and great fall foliage. Choose the 'inermis' variety so it is thornless!
- **When it Flowers:** Spring.
- **When to Plant:** Spring or fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Adaptable to poor soils. Low to moderate water.

### 12. Chinkapin Oak (Quercus muehlenbergii)
- **Summary:** Another excellent oak for Colorado. Has leaves that look more like chestnut leaves than typical oak leaves. Very tolerant of alkaline soils.
- **When it Flowers:** Spring (catkins).
- **When to Plant:** Spring or fall.
- **Care Guide:** Full sun. Well-drained soil. Drought tolerant once established.
