## Care Guide (<100 sqft with Drip Irrigation)

### Watering (Perforated 1/2" Tubes)
- **Establishment Phase:** If newly seeded, the top layer of soil needs to remain moist for 4-6 weeks for proper germination. Run the drip line more frequently (short durations) to keep the ground lightly damp but not flooded.
- **Established Phase:** Once mature, these native and adapted wildflowers are highly drought-tolerant. Run the drip system for a deep soak (30-45 minutes depending on emitter flow rate) 1-2 times a week during hot, dry spells. Allow the soil surface to dry out between waterings to encourage deep root growth and prevent rot.

### Weeding & Maintenance
- **Weeding:** Hand-pull weeds carefully. Early wildflower seedlings can look like weeds, so wait until you can confidently identify them (or wait for blooms) before pulling anything you aren't sure about. 
- **Deadheading:** You can pinch off spent flowers mid-summer to encourage a second flush of blooms.
- **Fall Cleanup:** **Do not cut back in the fall.** Leave the dead seedheads standing through the winter. This allows the plants to naturally re-seed the area for next year and provides essential winter food for local birds (like finches). Cut the dead stalks back to the ground in early spring just before new growth begins.

### Fertilizing
- Native wildflowers generally thrive in poor to average soil. Avoid heavy fertilizers, as too much nitrogen will encourage leafy weed growth and floppy, weak stems on the wildflowers. A light top-dressing of compost in the spring is sufficient.
