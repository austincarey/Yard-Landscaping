# Creeping Oregon Grape

**Scientific Name:** *Mahonia repens*
**Category:** Ground Cover
![[Pasted image 20260426145806.png]]

## Overview
A low-growing, evergreen native shrub that acts as a ground cover. It is virtually indestructible once established, thriving in deep shade and tolerating the alkaline soils of the Front Range. The holly-like leaflets turn a stunning maroon/purple in winter.

## Details
- **Light Requirements:** Part Shade to Full Shade
- **Water Needs:** Low to Moderate
- **Soil Tolerance:** Highly adaptable, tolerates alkaline clay soils well
- **Height:** 1 - 2 feet
- **Spread:** 2 - 3 feet (spreads via underground rhizomes)
- **Bloom Time:** April - May (fragrant yellow flower clusters)

## Care Guide
- **Planting:** Great for the most difficult "dry shade" spots beneath mature pines.
- **Watering:** Water regularly until established. Once established, it requires little supplemental irrigation.
- **Maintenance:** Very low maintenance. Prune in early spring only if necessary to remove damaged foliage.
- **Ecological Value:** One of the earliest food sources for native pollinators. Produces blue-black, grape-like berries highly prized by songbirds. Provides dense weed-resistant ground coverage.
