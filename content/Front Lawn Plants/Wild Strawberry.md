# Wild Strawberry

**Scientific Name:** *Fragaria virginiana*
**Category:** Ground Cover
![[Pasted image 20260426150821.png]]

## Overview
The native wild strawberry is a fast-growing, deciduous ground cover that is highly adaptable. It is particularly useful for filling in large areas quickly and handles the Front Range climate well.

## Details
- **Light Requirements:** Full Sun to Part Shade (prefers afternoon shade in Colorado)
- **Water Needs:** Moderate
- **Soil Tolerance:** Adaptable to various soil types, including clay
- **Height:** 4 - 6 inches
- **Spread:** 1 - 2 feet (spreads rapidly via stolons)
- **Bloom Time:** Spring (delicate white flowers)

## Care Guide
- **Planting:** Plant 12-18 inches apart to allow for rapid spreading.
- **Watering:** Requires slightly more moisture than xeric plants to fruit well, but is remarkably resilient. Can go dormant during extreme dry spells and rebound when watered.
- **Maintenance:** Can be aggressive if over-watered. Cut back runners if it spreads beyond its desired boundaries.
- **Ecological Value:** Produces intensely sweet, tiny strawberries that are a favorite for local wildlife. Flowers attract bees and other small pollinators.
