# Yard Seasonal Care Guide

This master guide provides a month-by-month breakdown of the specific care needed for the plants in your yard (Yard Landscaping). 

## Winter (December - February)
Winter in Colorado is dry. Your main focus is protecting plants from desiccation and doing dormant pruning.

- **Watering:** Provide "winter watering" on warm, dry days (above 40°F) to your newly planted items, young trees, and evergreens. 
- **Pruning (Dormant):**
  - Prune your **[[Ash Tree]]**, **[[Red Oak]]**, and **[[Siberian Crab Apple]]**. Pruning oaks in winter prevents Oak Wilt. 
  - Prune **[[Shrubby Cinquefoil]]** and **[[Common Snowberry]]** to maintain shape.
  - Cut back **[[Feather Reed Grass]]** to 2-3 inches above the ground before new green shoots appear in late winter.
  - Cut back **[[Asian Virginsbower]]** (Sweet Autumn Clematis) down to about 12 inches.
- **Weed Control:** N/A

## Early Spring (March - April)
The soil thaws and the growing season begins. It's time to prep the beds.

- **Cleanup:** Remove old foliage from perennials like **[[Hairy Alumroot]]**, **[[Daylily]]**, and **[[Columbine]]**.
- **Pruning:**
  - Prune your **[[China Rose]]** just as the leaf buds begin to swell. 
  - Do *not* prune spring-flowering shrubs yet!
- **Fertilizing:** Apply tree fertilizer to your **[[Silver Maple]]** and **[[Linden Tree]]**. Apply a light balanced fertilizer or compost to the perennial beds.
- **Vegetables:** Sow cool-weather crops like **[[Garden Lettuce]]**, **[[Carrot]]**, and **[[Parsley]]** as soon as the soil is workable.

## Late Spring (May)
The danger of frost passes. Explosive growth happens now.

- **Pruning (Post-Bloom):** 
  - Immediately *after* they finish flowering, prune your **[[Common Lilac]]**, **[[Chinese Snowball]]**, **[[Elm-leaf Spiraea]]**, and **[[Weigela]]**.
  - Shear back **[[Moss Phlox]]** and **[[Basket of Gold]]** by 1/3 after they finish blooming to keep them compact.
- **Planting/Dividing:** Plant your **[[Summer Squash]]** once soil warms. Divide fall-blooming perennials like **[[New England Aster]]** if clumps are too large.
- **Weed Control:** Aggressively hand-pull or spot-treat **[[Lady's Thumb]]**, **[[Field Bindweed]]**, and **[[Japanese Knotweed]]** shoots as soon as they appear.

## Summer (June - August)
Heat sets in. Focus is on watering, deadheading, and monitoring for pests.

- **Watering:** Ensure the **[[Summer Squash]]** and vegetables receive consistent moisture.
- **Maintenance (Deadheading):**
  - Continuously deadhead your **[[Pincushion Flower]]**, **[[Garden Cosmos]]**, **[[Purple Coneflower]]**, and **[[China Rose]]** to force them to produce more flowers.
  - Cut back **[[Serbian Bellflower]]** and **[[Bloody Cranesbill]]** if they get leggy to encourage a second flush of leaves/blooms.
- **Pest Control:** 
  - Monitor the **[[Ash Tree]]** heavily for Emerald Ash Borer signs (D-shaped exit holes, canopy dieback).
  - Watch for Iron Chlorosis (yellow leaves with green veins) on the **[[Red Oak]]**.
- **Weed Control:** Do not let **[[Mullein]]**, **[[Prickly Lettuce]]**, or **[[Goosefoot]]** go to seed. Apply systemic herbicides to **[[Field Bindweed]]** and **[[Japanese Knotweed]]** in late summer when the plant is drawing nutrients down into its roots.

## Fall (September - November)
Preparing the garden for winter dormancy.

- **Planting/Dividing:** 
  - Plant **[[Garden Tulip]]** bulbs before the ground freezes.
  - Divide spring/summer blooming perennials like **[[Peony]]** and **[[Daylily]]**.
- **Cleanup:** 
  - After the first hard frost, cut back the dead foliage of **[[Peony]]** and discard it in the trash (not compost) to prevent fungal diseases like botrytis.
  - Leave the seed heads on your **[[Purple Coneflower]]** and **[[Black-eyed Susan]]** for winter foraging birds.
- **Weed Control:** Final application of systemic herbicide on stubborn **[[Japanese Knotweed]]** or **[[Field Bindweed]]** patches before the first hard freeze.

## Lawn Care Guide (Scott's Program)
For your cool-season grasses (**[[Kentucky Bluegrass]]** and **[[Tall Fescue]]**), here is the standard Scott's 4-step year-round recommendation:

- **Early Spring (Feb - April):** Apply *Scotts Halts Crabgrass Preventer with Lawn Food*. This stops **[[Crabgrass]]** and **[[Spurge]]** before they germinate while greening up the lawn.
- **Late Spring (April - June):** Apply *Scotts Weed Control plus Lawn Food*. This kills actively growing broadleaf weeds like **[[Dandelion]]** and **[[Plantain]]** and provides a second feeding.
- **Summer (June - August):** Apply *Scotts SummerGuard Lawn Food* (or similar summer fertilizer). This helps the grass withstand intense Colorado heat. *Note: Lawns need about 1.5 - 2 inches of water per week during peak heat.*
- **Fall (September - November):** Apply *Scotts Turf Builder WinterGuard Fall Lawn Food*. This is the most critical feeding of the year to build deep, strong roots for winter. It's also the best time to aerate the lawn.
